\section{Soundness of \pmt}\label{soundness section}

\par This section is dedicated to proving the soundness of $\pmt$. We need to prove that $\Gamma \vdash_\pmt \phi$ implies $\Gamma\Vdash \phi$. So we may assume $\Gamma \vdash_\pmt \phi$. I.e., when any saturated tableau with $\{\neg\phi:x\}\cup\{\gamma_i:x\mid\gamma_i\in\Gamma\}$ in the root, is closed. This means that, according to Definition~\ref{labelled tableau def}, each branch contains both $\psi:y$ and $\neg\psi:y$ or it contains $\neg[\iot{0}]:y$ for some $\psi\in\Lp, y\in\NAMES$. Now, to prove soundness, we have to prove that what is in the root, i.e., $\Gamma \cup \{\neg \phi\}$, is also not satisfiable. So conversely, we can prove soundness by proving that if $\Gamma \cup \{\neg \phi\}$ is satisfiable, then at least one of the leaf nodes is satisfiable.


\begin{theorem}\label{Soundness of PMT - theorem}
    $\pmt$ is sound. I.e., $\Gamma \vdash_\pmt \phi$ implies $\Gamma\Vdash \phi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    \par To prove the soundness of $\pmt$, we prove that each rule preserves satisfiability from top to bottom. Therefore if we have $\Gamma \vdash_\pmt \phi$ and our rules preserve satisfiability, then $\Gamma\Vdash \phi$.

\par So let's prove the soundness of each rule. Note, Definition~\ref{satisfiability clauses - pure} and Definition~\ref{sat of Gamma pure} will be used in each of these proofs. We let $\M = (W,\{ R_{\alpha} \}_{\mtt},V)$ be an arbitrary $\mtt$-model.


\begin{center}
\renewcommand*\linenumberstyle[1]{}

Negation Elimination: \\
\begin{tableau}
    {}
    [\Delta \coms \neg\neg\phi :x
    [\Delta \coms \phi: x ,just={$(\neg E)$}]]
\end{tableau}
\end{center}\

\par Assume $\Delta \cup \{\neg\neg\phi:x\}$ is satisfied in $\M$. We need to show that $\phi:x$ is satisfied in $\M$. So since $\{\neg\neg\phi:x\}$ is satisfied in $\M$, then $\M,\mathsf{S}(x) \Vdash \neg\neg\phi$, then $\M,\mathsf{S}(x) \not\Vdash \neg\phi$. Therefore $\M,\mathsf{S}(x) \Vdash \phi$. Thus $\Delta \cup \{\phi:x\}$ is satisfied in $\M$.

\par Let $\Delta' = \Delta \cup \{\neg\lsb\alpha\rsb(\phi_1\text{
    ,\,} \phi_2\coms \ldots\coms \phi_{m}):x\}$ and $\Delta'' = \Delta \cup \{R_\alpha xy_1\ldots y_m \coms \neg\phi_1 :y_1 \coms \ldots \coms \neg\phi_m :y_m\}$.
\par Now, assume $\Delta'$ is satisfied in $\M$. This means that $\M, \mathsf{S}(x) \Vdash \neg[\alpha](\phi_1,\phi_2, \ldots, \phi_{m})$. Then $\M, \mathsf{S}(x) \not\Vdash [\alpha](\phi_1,\phi_2, \ldots, \phi_{m})$. I.e., it is not the case that for all $z_1,\ldots, z_m \in W$ with $R_\alpha^{\M}(\mathsf{S}(x)z_1\ldots z_m)$ we have $\M,z_1\Vdash \phi_1$ or $\M,z_2\Vdash \phi_2$ or $\cdots$ or $\M,z_m\Vdash \phi_m$. Therefore, we have that there exists $z_1\ldots z_m \in W$ with $R_\alpha^{\M}(\mathsf{S}(x)z_1\ldots z_m)$ such that $\M,z_1\not\Vdash \phi_1$ and $\M,z_2\not\Vdash \phi_2$ and $\cdots$ and $\M,z_m\not\Vdash \phi_m$. I.e., there exists $z_1\ldots z_m \in W$ with $R_\alpha^{\M}(\mathsf{S}(x)z_1\ldots z_m)$ such that $\M,z_1\Vdash \neg\phi_1$ and $\M,z_2\Vdash \neg\phi_2$ and $\cdots$ and $\M,z_m\Vdash \neg\phi_m$.
\par Now to prove that $\M$ satisfies $\Delta''$ we must find mappings $\mathsf{S}'$ and $\mathsf{R}'$ such that it satisfies Definition~\ref{sat of Gamma pure}. To do this, we let $\mathsf{R}'\rvert_{\text{RNames}(\Delta')}= \mathsf{R}\rvert_{\text{RNames}(\Delta')}$ and set $\mathsf{R'}(R_\alpha) = R_\alpha^\M$.

We also let $\mathsf{S}'$ be such that: $\mathsf{S}'\rvert_{\text{Names}(\Delta')}= \mathsf{S}\rvert_{\text{Names}(\Delta')}$ and set $\mathsf{S}'(y_i) = z_i$ for $1\leq i \leq m$, since all $y_i$'s are new. Using $\mathsf{R}'$ and $\mathsf{S}'$, we can conclude $\M$ satisfies $\Delta''$ and hence the rule is sound.

\par This rule branches, so to prove soundness, we need to show that assuming the premise is satisfied in $\M$ results in at least one of the conclusions being satisfied in $\M$.
\par Assume $\Delta \cup \{\lsb\alpha\rsb(\phi_1\coms \phi_2\coms \ldots\coms \phi_{m}):x\coms R_\alpha xy_1\ldots y_m\}$ is satisfied in $\M$. Thus $\M, \mathsf{S}(x) \Vdash [\alpha](\phi_1,\phi_2,\ldots,\phi_m)$, and since we also have $R_\alpha(xy_1\ldots y_m)$ then, $(\mathsf{S}(x),\mathsf{S}(y_1),\ldots,\mathsf{S}(y_m)) \in R_\alpha^{\M}$, i.e., $R_\alpha^{\M}(\mathsf{S}(x)\mathsf{S}(y_1)\ldots\mathsf{S}(y_m))$. Therefore, we have $\M, \mathsf{S}(y_1) \Vdash \phi_1$ or $\M, \mathsf{S}(y_2)\Vdash \phi_2$ or $\cdots$ or $ \M, \mathsf{S}(y_m)\Vdash \phi_m$. And so, at least one of these is true, hence the rule is sound.

\par Assume $\Delta \cup \{R_{\iota_n}(x_0\ldots x_n) \coms \phi :x_i\}$ ($0\leq i\leq n$) is satisfied in $\M$. Then we need to show that \{$\phi:x_j$\} $(0\leq j\leq n, i\not= j)$ is satisfied in $\M$. Now by assumption, we have $\M, \mathsf{S}(x_i) \Vdash \phi$, we also have $R_{\iota_n}(x_0\ldots x_n)$ so this means that $(\mathsf{S}(x_0),\ldots,\mathsf{S}(x_n)) \in R_{\iota_n}^{\M} = \{(w,\ldots,w)\mid w\in W\}$. Therefore $\mathsf{S}(x_0) = \cdots =\mathsf{S}(x_n)$. So, $\M, \mathsf{S}(x_j) \Vdash \phi$.

 $(\mathsf{S}(b_0),\mathsf{S}(b_1),\ldots,\mathsf{S}(x_i),
 \\ \ldots,\mathsf{S}(b_m)) \in R_\alpha^{\M}$, and $(\mathsf{S}(x_0),\ldots,\mathsf{S}(x_n)) \in R_{\iota_n}^{\M}$. 
 Therefore $\mathsf{S}(x_0) = \cdots =\mathsf{S}(x_n)$. So, $(\mathsf{S}(b_0),\mathsf{S}(b_1),
 \\ \ldots,\mathsf{S}(x_j),\ldots,\mathsf{S}(b_m)) \in R_\alpha^{\M}$ and thus $\{R_\alpha(b_0b_1\ldots x_j \ldots b_{m})\}$ is satisfied in $\M$.

$\Delta \cup \{R_{\alpha(\beta_1, \beta_2, \ldots, \beta_m)}(xx_1^1x_2^1\ldots x_{\rho(\beta_1)}^1 x_1^2 \ldots x_{\rho(\beta_2)}^2 \ldots x_1^m \ldots x_{\rho(\beta_m)}^m)\}$ = $\Delta'$ and

\par Now we claim that $\Delta''$ is satisfied in $\M$. From the assumption we have that $\Delta$ is satisfied in $\M$, to show that $\{R_{\alpha}(xy_1 \ldots y_m) \coms R_{\beta_1}(y_1x_1^1 \ldots x_{\rho(\beta_1)}^1) \coms \ldots \coms R_{\beta_m}(y_m x_1^m \ldots x_{\rho(\beta_m)}^m)\}$ is satisfied in $\M$ we create mappings $\mathsf{R}'$ and $\mathsf{S}'$. So, we let $\mathsf{R}'= \mathsf{R}$. We can do this because in Definition~\ref{RNames function definition} of RNames if $\alpha(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m)\in\RnamD$ then $\alpha,\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m\in\RnamD$. We also let $\mathsf{S}'$ be such that: $\mathsf{S}'\rvert_{\text{Names}(\Delta')}= \mathsf{S}\rvert_{\text{Names}(\Delta')}$ and set $\mathsf{S}'(y_i) = z_i$ for $1\leq i \leq m$, since all $y_i$'s are new. Using $\mathsf{R}'$ and $\mathsf{S}'$, we can conclude $\M$ satisfies $\Delta''$ and hence the rule is sound.
